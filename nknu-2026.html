02 · The Moment

The Moment

The image made no sense.

In an October 2024 junior-high information-technology class, a student with an intellectual disability wanted AI to draw “a cat under a tree.” The result was visually incoherent. The student lowered their head and stopped trying.

That moment became the starting point of this study: the obstacle was not access to AI, but the language needed to communicate an idea to it.

03 · Problem Framing

Problem Framing

Students did not lack AI skills.
They lacked a way to say what they imagined.

Ethics

04 · Methodology

Methodology

ADDIE + IR

Before the standard ADDIE sequence, the study adds an original Implementation Review stage. Real classroom difficulties from 2024 are systematically converted into design requirements.

IRImplementation Review
AAnalysis
DDesign
DDevelopment
I / EImplementation & Evaluation

The logic borrows from backward design: start with authentic classroom pain points rather than forcing a finished plan into the classroom.

05 · Prompt Scaffolding

Prompt Scaffolding

The five-element prompt formula

Type

Watercolor, comic, photographic, or other visual form.

Subject

Who or what appears, including key traits and actions.

Environment

Where the scene takes place and what surrounds it.

Composition

Scale, viewpoint, framing, and image division.

Atmosphere

Light, mood, time of day, and emotional tone.

06 · Design Move

Design Move

Choose, remove, and refine — instead of inventing every word.

Students selected options from a visual menu and deleted what they did not want. This reduced writing pressure while preserving authorship and decision-making.

Intellectual disability

Visual cue cards supported word recognition and reduced the writing burden.

ADHD

Tasks were divided into clear 10–15 minute segments with visible steps.

General education

Advanced challenges and open-ended creation preserved autonomy and depth.

07 · Finding I

Finding I

When the barrier disappeared, self-efficacy returned.

“The light in the student’s eyes reflected a new confirmation of their own ability.”

The digital menu supported more complete visual narratives. Successful generation experiences gave students concrete evidence that their ideas could become visible.

08 · Finding II

Finding II

Immediate visual feedback changed attention.

<10 minTypical sustained attention in conventional tasks
20–30 minObserved sustained attention during AI image creation
5–10 secApproximate generation-feedback cycle
FlowRepeated trying without additional behavior management

The study interprets the short feedback loop as a possible trigger for deeper engagement among students with ADHD, while treating the finding as classroom evidence rather than a causal claim.
