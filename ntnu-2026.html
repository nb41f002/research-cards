02·Background

Background

Context

The researcher coordinated and taught an elementary bilingual camp while co-designing preparation, badge and certificate tools with AI.

Focus

Requests and revisions record decisions grounded in curriculum documents, role responsibilities and conditions of use.

Purpose

The study examines clarification, evaluation and revision to understand teacher professional agency in collaboration.

03·Research Questions

Research questions

Q1

What patterns of clarification, correction and redesign occur in teacher–AI collaboration?

Q2

What professional criteria guide the evaluation and rewriting of AI output?

Q3

How do teacher constraints alter functions, information architecture and delivery, and what do these processes suggest for teacher roles and professional culture?

Questions follow the submitted manuscript.

04·Method

Method

01
Design

Instrumental case study using a reflexive thematic analysis approach.

02
Setting

April–July 2026; one researcher serving as coordinator, instructor and system designer.

03
Sources

Eight Claude threads, including post-camp reflection, with Claude Design text records and lesson plans.

04
Analysis

Trace the problem, AI proposal, researcher judgment, revision account and subsequent response; compare reasons across events.

05·Findings

Findings

Four candidate themes were developed: curriculum documents as a basis for content, responsibilities and decision boundaries, turning on-site conditions into functional rules, and continued negotiation of adoption and verification.

Curriculum and responsibilities
Slide sequences and formal scripts guided revisions; the coordinator queried missing schedules and set the order of production.
Conditions become rules
Printing, device use and access to typhoon contingency plans became concrete functions and information arrangements.
Verification after adoption
Feedback from use led to additional requirements, revised proposals and renewed investigation of problems.
06·Limitations

Limitations

  • —One researcher and one camp context; the researcher’s own roles shaped event selection and interpretation.
  • —Some Claude Design records lack message-level timestamps; cross-platform conversations and successive artifacts have preservation gaps.
  • —The primary evidence traces discussion and revision. Classroom use is described through post-camp reflection and researcher experience.

09·Teaching implications

Preserving reasons for revisions makes professional judgment available for collegial discussion.

  • · Check generated content against formal curriculum materials.
  • · Express work requirements as usable functions.
  • · Retain feedback and subsequent revisions as teacher-education case materials.
