Does a Learning Gap Remain under Generative-AI Support? A Quantitative Educational-Equity Analysis across Junior-High Technology Courses

Authors: Jung-Chen Yeh and Chia-Yi Lee
Southern Taiwan University of Science and Technology · 2026 International Conference on Interdisciplinary AI and Digital Humanities · October 2, 2026
02 · The Premise

The Premise

We often say AI can advance educational equity.
Has anyone measured it in an actual classroom?

Large-sample quantitative comparisons between students with special educational needs and general-education students remain limited. This study tests the assumption using two semesters of classroom data.

03 · Three Indicators

Three Indicators

Turn “equity” into measurable questions.

Foundational skill

Did students’ Chinese typing speed improve?

Creative participation

Did assignment submission improve across semesters?

Group difference

After prior performance is controlled, does a SEN–general gap remain?

04 · Methodology

Methodology

Two semesters — not a perception survey.

495Complete participants across grades 7–8
32Students with special educational needs · 6.5%
18Classes in one public junior high school
3Data sources: typing tests, scores, submissions

Paired-samples t tests, an exact McNemar test, and ANCOVA were used. Semester-I AI-image scores served as a covariate for prior AI-creation performance before comparing SUNO outcomes.

05 · Intervention

Intervention

Two semesters, two scaffolding systems.

Semester I · AI Images

A prompt formula and five-element cue cards translated unclear ideas into structured language.

Semester II · SUNO Music

A three-tier cognitive scaffold supported concept generation, prompt construction, and interactive revision.

Longitudinal continuity

The two-semester sequence made prior AI-creation performance available as a covariate.

06 · Result I

Result I

Chinese typing: 23.9 → 26.8 characters/min.

08 · Result III

Result III

The adjusted group difference was not statistically significant.

General education

Raw mean 74.69 · adjusted mean 74.62

SEN students

Raw mean 70.97 · adjusted mean 72.00

Adjusted difference

2.62 points · p = .147

The raw gap was 3.72 points. After controlling for prior AI-image performance, it narrowed to 2.62 points and did not reach statistical significance.

A non-significant difference does not establish equivalence or prove that a gap disappeared. Raw and adjusted differences are not a pre/post comparison of the same outcome.

09 · The Number That Matters

The Number That Matters

100%

All 32 SEN students completed a work.
No score was below 60.

In creative courses, non-submission is often the most visible form of exclusion. In this cohort, nobody was left behind at that threshold.

10 · Limitations

Limitations

What this study cannot claim.

No non-scaffold control

Other concurrent instructional factors cannot be excluded.

No homogeneous outcome pretest

AI images and SUNO music are different task types.

No causal conclusion

The study cannot yet say that GenAI or scaffolding caused the gap to shrink.

The contribution is preliminary quantitative evidence, not a causal claim.

11 · The Heart

Foundational skill improved. Creative participation improved. After prior performance was controlled, the two groups did not differ significantly — measured evidence for inclusive GenAI creation.
