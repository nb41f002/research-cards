Does a Learning Gap Remain under Generative-AI Support? A Quantitative Educational-Equity Analysis across Junior-High Technology Courses

Graduate student: Yeh, Jung-Chen · Advisor: Li, Chia-Yi
Southern Taiwan University of Science and Technology · 2026 International Conference on Interdisciplinary AI and Digital Humanities · October 2, 2026
02 · The Premise

The Premise

We often say AI can advance educational equity.
Has anyone measured it in an actual classroom?

Large-sample quantitative comparisons between students with special educational needs and general-education students remain limited. This study looks at two semesters of classroom records.

Three Indicators

Work and prompt records

Group outcomes

Records include Chinese typing, course scores, and traceable student works across two semesters.

05 · Intervention

Intervention

Support across two semesters.

Semester I · AI Images

A prompt formula and five-element cue cards turned unclear ideas into structured language.

Semester II · SUNO Music

A three-tier scaffold: theme frames, a prompt-building website, and interactive revision.

Same students

Six lessons per unit; each task is graded by its own course rubric.

09 · The Number That Matters

Scores of exactly 60

25.8%

of SEN students scored exactly 60,
compared with 11.6% of general students.

A score of exactly 60 means a song was submitted with the prompt left blank or copied from the template. Prompts are where SEN students needed more support.

10 · Three Students

One scaffold, three starting points

Three students, three ways in.

A · Special educational needs

Writing is difficult for this student. With picture cards and an aide, the student wrote “a cat under the tree on the right” and “a bird flying in the sky, autumn”.

B · ADHD

Usually distracted within 5–8 minutes; stayed focused for more than 20 minutes on this task and generated three images from one prompt.

C · Home-schooled

Studies at home, yet attended the technology class every week during the AI-image unit.

11 · Scope

Scope

Where the evidence comes from.

One school

Two semesters of technology courses in one public junior high school, without a non-scaffold comparison group.

Two task types

AI images and SUNO music are graded by their own rubrics.

Next step

Testing effects requires a comparison design or matched pre/post measures.

This study offers a quantitative description of one school setting.

12 · The Heart

Read the three records separately. Give prompts more support. One scaffold can offer many ways into creative work.
