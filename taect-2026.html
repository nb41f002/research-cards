02·Background

Background

Context

Twenty elementary students in Grades 2–6 attended the camp in two age-based classes, with local food culture and story quests connecting activities.

Focus

The teacher specified listening, starting, pausing and tidying functions before embedding call-and-response phrases in the chorus and video.

Purpose

Teaching records describe design and participation; Days 4–5 video adds individual action observations.

03·Research Questions

Research questions

Q1

How were the AI song and multimodal scaffolds designed and embedded in camp activities?

Q2

How did responses to four classroom-English routines and participation develop over five days?

Q3

Supplementary analysis: where did AI tools and human action classifications agree or differ?

Questions follow the submitted manuscript.

04·Method

Method

01
Design

Teaching-practice case study combining qualitative process analysis and descriptive action coding.

02
Setting

20 regular students: 13 in the younger group and 7 in the older group.

03
Sources

Meeting records, teaching handbook, schedules, routine cards, song, video, photos and original Days 4–5 recordings.

04
Analysis

20 students × 4 routines × 10 rounds = 800 human-coded observations; 160 paired observations for two-rater agreement.

05·Findings

Findings

Teaching records describe a progression from watching and imitation to coordinated singing and dancing. Formal action results use human judgments of the original video.

605 observable responses
551 of 605 observable responses showed clearly completed actions (91.1%); 195 of all 800 observations were unobservable.
160 paired observations
Separate coding of the first two rounds on Day 5 yielded 90.6% agreement and Cohen’s κ = .831.
AI–human comparison
Agreement ranged from 55.5% to 71.8%. Now showed the most disagreements, mainly concerning observability.
06·Limitations

Limitations

  • —One five-day camp; the 800 observations repeatedly measure the same 20 students.
  • —Camera position, occlusion and framing affect observability; 195 unobservable responses are reported separately.
  • —Pronunciation and English comprehension require appropriate additional measures. AI classifications and human results are stored separately.

09·Teaching implications

Use a small set of phrases, repeated responses and consistent gestures in everyday classroom activity.

  • · Define the classroom purpose of each routine.
  • · Coordinate audio, subtitles, demonstration and gestures.
  • · Report observable denominators and retain human confirmation of action results.
